A **channel** is a way in and out of Devic that is not the API. A message arrives where the person already is — their inbox, their Slack, their phone — starts or continues a run, and the answer goes back the same way.

There are three, and they are set up once here and then attached to individual agents and assistants.

![Channels](/images/admin/channels.png)

<Columns cols={3}>
  <Card title="Email" icon="envelope">

  </Card>
  <Card title="Slack" icon="slack">
    A bot in a workspace. It answers where it is mentioned and in the threads it is part of.
  </Card>
  <Card title="WhatsApp" icon="whatsapp">
    A business number. Someone writes to it and an assistant answers in the chat.
  </Card>

<Note>
  Setting a channel up is one thing; deciding **who answers on it** is another. The presence lives here, in Configuration; the binding — which agent or assistant listens, and how it replies — lives on that entity's own **Channels** page.
</Note>

## Email

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Gmail or Outlook" icon="envelope">
    One click. Authorise once and both sending and receiving are configured for you, with the right hosts, ports and OAuth.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Any other provider" icon="server">
    Configure SMTP and IMAP by hand: host, port, TLS and credentials.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Note>
  Gmail and Outlook have retired or restricted basic authentication, which is why the guided flow exists. If you are configuring either of them by hand and hitting authentication failures, use the native connection instead.
</Note>

Devic can use its own registered application, or yours — bring your own OAuth client when your organisation requires the consent to be granted to an app you control.

### Aliases

A Gmail account can send as any of its configured aliases, and mail arriving at an alias is picked up as belonging to that mailbox. This is what lets one connected account serve `support@`, `billing@` and `hello@` without three mailboxes.

### Incoming mail

- **Threading** follows the standard mail headers, so a reply lands in the conversation it belongs to rather than opening a new one.
- **The tenant** is resolved from the sender, so a customer writing in is attributed to their own tenant automatically.

### When a connection breaks

Tokens expire and passwords get changed. A mailbox that can no longer authenticate is marked as needing re-authorisation rather than failing silently, so the fix is one click and not an investigation into why an agent stopped answering.

## Slack

A Slack app is registered once and then **installed** into as many workspaces as you like — your own, and your customers'. Each installation is a workspace that can reach the same bot.

Everything the bot may do is the scopes it asks for on the consent screen, and it only answers where it is invited: a channel it has been added to, a thread it is part of, a direct message.

### Inviting a customer

You cannot install a bot into somebody else's Slack, and they should not have to sign into Devic to do it. So you issue them a **link**: it names the tenant it belongs to, they open it, they consent inside their own Slack, and the workspace that comes back is mapped to that tenant.

See [Connecting a customer](#connecting-a-customer) below.

## WhatsApp

A WhatsApp number is either **connected** — you already have one, and bring it — or **bought** from inside Devic, in any of 68 countries. Either way the business account behind it stays yours, and Meta bills its templates to you.

<Warning>
  Connecting a WhatsApp number you already use redirects its webhooks immediately and with no overlap. If it is currently feeding another integration, that integration stops receiving messages the moment you connect.
</Warning>

### The 24-hour window

This is Meta's rule, not ours, and it shapes what an assistant can do:

- **Within a day of the person's last message**, anything can be sent freely.
- **After that**, only an approved template — or a "utility" send, if Meta has approved the business for it.

An answer that cannot go out is **not discarded**. It waits, and goes out the moment the person writes again, ahead of whatever they just said. The binding decides what happens in the meantime: send a utility message, nudge with an approved template, or simply hold.

### What arrives

Photos and documents are stored and handed to the model; **voice notes are transcribed** and reach the assistant as text, marked as spoken rather than typed. A quote-reply arrives with the message it answers.

Every message is tagged with the channel it came in on, so an assistant serving several can tell which one it is reading.

### The number's own profile

The picture, the description, the address and the websites people see when they open the chat are edited from the number's card and apply immediately. **The display name is the exception**: Meta reviews it, which takes one to three business days, and the chat keeps the old name until it is approved.

## Connecting a customer

Slack and WhatsApp both answer the same question — *whose message is this?* — and both answer it the same way: with a link that names one of your tenants before it is sent.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Issue the link">
    From the tenant's **Channels** panel, or from your own backend through the API. The tenant is frozen into the link when it is made.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They open it">
    Slack shows them a consent screen. WhatsApp shows them a QR, a button that opens the chat with a short code already written, and the code on its own.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They connect">
    In Slack, by installing the bot. In WhatsApp, **by sending that message** — there is nothing to install, and the message itself is the proof that the number is theirs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It is attributed">
    From then on every message from that workspace or that person carries your `tenantId`, and shows up in that tenant's costs, usage and limits like everything else.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Whoever holds the link decides **whether** to connect, never **to whom**. That is what makes it safe to send through an email, a ticket or a chat message.
</Note>

Revoking a link stops it working. It does **not** unmap what it already connected — that is a separate and deliberate act, from the directory.

### Who is talking without an owner

Not everyone arrives through a link. The **directory** lists every workspace, number and person that has been seen, including the ones mapped to nobody yet, so you can attach them by hand.

A line that should only answer people you have registered can be set to say so: an unknown number gets one fixed sentence, in the language of its own country code, and no run starts.
